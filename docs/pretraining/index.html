Each lesson runs Puzzle → Explore → Name → Challenge (interactive-educator method), for a learner who knows linear algebra, softmax and basic neural networks but is new to NLP. It follows the Tokenization Lab; the deck's GPT-1 and KV-cache slides (90–107) are taught in the GPT Lab. Source deck: M. Samiei, "Lecture 5: Pretraining", CS 677, Sharif University of Technology (after Bengio et al. 2003, Devlin et al. 2019, Liu et al. 2019, Ardakani 2022). From the slides, reproduced exactly: the 512 → 2 head (slide 13); the oracle values 0.4 × 0.2 × 0.5 × 0.1 = 0.004 and 0.0000002 (26–27), cup 55% / mug 25% / glass 10% (25) and positive 0.72 (28); the eight-sentence corpus, its counts and the scores 0.225, 0.150 and 0 (35–39, 44); models A and B, H ≈ 0.474 / 0.749 and (1/2 · 1/4 · 1/8)^(−1/3) = 4 (40–43); add-one P(runs | cat) = 1/10 (46); loss 2.303 → 0.511 and the Brown perplexities 312 / 268 / 252 (55–56); 15% with 80/10/10 and the losses 1.609 + 0.223 (70, 75); the GLUE accuracies 68.1/84.9, 81.4/93.2, 54.9/72.2 (81) and the NSP ablations 84.4/83.9 and 84.0/84.7 (83). Everything else is a teaching toy: the 2-D leaf data, toy backbone and its accuracy curves (lesson 1), the oracle's other probabilities (2), the extra corpus sentences (3), the 2-D neural LM trained in your browser and its probabilities (5), the masked-word guesses, the 16-word masking sentence and the challenge input (6), and the head outputs in the tagging and QA examples (7).
